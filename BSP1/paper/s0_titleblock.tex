{Limits to Arbitrage in the Indian Equity Market: Theory, Data and the Futures-Cash Band}
{Prince Kumar \quad{\color{muted}\normalsize\mdseries Supervisor: Prof.\ Aditi Chaubal}}

\begin{paperabstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
A stock future should trade at the price of the stock plus the cost of carrying it to expiry; otherwise an
arbitrageur could buy the cheaper of the two and sell the dearer. Trading costs, short-sale constraints, limited capital
and position limits make this arbitrage costly, so the gap between the two, the basis, can persist. In this first stage
of my BS project I study the basis of single-stock futures in India from 2015 to 2026. I build a model in which each
Indian limit to arbitrage is a parameter, including the F\&O ban, under which every trader may only reduce positions once
open interest nears a stock's position limit, and I fix the tests before relating any outcome to any treatment. The
basis moves in a wide, asymmetric band, from about 35 basis points below each stock's median to about 24 above it, and
the full cost of a new cash-and-carry position lies in its outer tail. The upper part of the basis rose after the 2024 and
2026 tax increases, as predicted, but by more than the cost changes and by amounts that are not unusual on other dates.
The ban binds hard on trading: futures volume falls by about 87\%, and under intraday enforcement every futures trade in a
ban closed existing positions.
